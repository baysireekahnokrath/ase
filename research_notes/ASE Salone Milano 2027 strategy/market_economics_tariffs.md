## 1. Furniture demand outlook 2026–2027 (EU, US, Middle East, Japan, Australia; upholstery)

### Takeaway
The global market is flat. CSIL's June 2026 outlook expects world furniture consumption to stagnate in real terms in 2026, with only a marginal recovery in 2027. Europe is close to stagnant, Italy's own export engine is shrinking (-3.9% Jan–Jul 2026), and US demand is flat and depends on housing turnover. Growth is in contract and hospitality, not retail volume.

### Cited Findings
- **World (CSIL, June 2026 edition, F):** after a hard 2025, world furniture consumption across 100 countries is expected to "stagnate in 2026 in real terms, with a marginal recovery in 2027". International furniture trade was almost flat in 2025, and the effects of the tariffs imposed in October 2025 were expected to show up in 2026. CSIL assumes world GDP growth of 3.1% in 2026 and 3.2% in 2027, and names the Middle East war and geopolitics as major risks. — [CSIL World Furniture Outlook 2026–2027](https://www.worldfurnitureonline.com/report/world-furniture-outlook/); [Canadian Interiors, Jun 2026](https://www.canadianinteriors.com/2026/06/26/heightened-uncertainty-continues-to-affect-furniture-market-csil-report/)
- **Earlier CSIL forecasts (Nov 2025, F)** put 2026 world consumption growth at about +1%. A CSIL European report summary gave +1.2% for 2026 and +1.4% for 2027. These conflict with the later June 2026 "stagnation" call, so the newest edition should take precedence. — [Canadian Interiors, Nov 2025](https://www.canadianinteriors.com/2025/11/21/ongoing-uncertainty-continues-to-negatively-affect-furniture-sector-csil-report/); [Meble Polska](https://meblepolska.pl/en/news/european-furniture-industry-2026-report-by-csil/); [Canadian Interiors, Feb 2026](https://www.canadianinteriors.com/2026/02/03/trade-uncertainty-weighs-on-europes-furniture-market-csil-report/)
- **Europe (CSIL, F):** demand is forecast to be almost stagnant in 2026. Households keep prioritising savings, and spending is shifting to experiences rather than durables. — [Canadian Interiors, Feb 2026](https://www.canadianinteriors.com/2026/02/03/trade-uncertainty-weighs-on-europes-furniture-market-csil-report/)
- **Italy (FederlegnoArredo, A):** the wood-furniture sector closed 2025 up 1.4% at €52.5bn (Salone 2026 economic note). Exports in full-year 2025 were stable at +0.4%, over €19.3bn. — [FLA economic note, Salone 2026 (PDF)](https://areapress.salonemilano.it/26_08_april_en/Economic_Note_FederlegnoArredo_64th_edition_Salone_del_Mobile_Milano.pdf); [Xylon, Oct 2026](https://www.xylon.it/en/2026/10/27/federlegnoarredo-export-in-calo-del-39-nei-primi-sette-mesi-del-2026/)
- **Italy 2026 YTD (A):** wood-furniture exports fell 3.9% in Jan–Jul 2026. The main declines were France (-4.4%), the US (-9.4%) and Germany (-7.4%), together a loss of more than €288m. Exports to the Middle East fell 48.8% in March 2026 alone, at the time of the regional conflict. Q1 production volumes were down 5.6%, partly offset by higher producer prices. — [Agenzia Nova](https://www.agenzianova.com/en/news/imprese-federlegnoarredo-nei-primi-sette-mesi-2026-export-39-per-cento/); [Xylon](https://www.xylon.it/en/2026/10/27/federlegnoarredo-export-in-calo-del-39-nei-primi-sette-mesi-del-2026/); [Interior Daily](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9884038/us-and-german-demand-drags-italian-furniture-exports-down-by-5-4/); [Furnishing Idea](https://www.furnishingidea.com/article/federlegnoarredo-furniture-exports-open-2026-in-negative-territory-with-a-significant-contraction-toward-the-united-states)
- **Italy upholstery structure (CSIL 2026 report listing):** nearly three-quarters of Italian upholstered production is exported, led by France, the US and the UK. Domestic consumption is modest and partly supplied by imports, mainly from China, Romania and a fast-rising Serbia. — [CSIL, Upholstered furniture industry in Italy](https://www.worldfurnitureonline.com/report/the-upholstered-furniture-industry-in-italy/)
- **US:** the industry entered 2026 with muted demand, higher inventories and cost pressure (Smith Leonard via Interior Daily). Furniture store sales were down in H1 2026. Realtor.com projected existing-home sales up only 1.7% in 2026, with mortgage rates around 6.3% (F). Zelman expects housing activity to grow 5–6% in 2026–27 (F). IBISWorld puts US furniture store revenue at $166.8bn in 2026. — [Interior Daily](https://interiordaily.com/article/9833448/us-furniture-market-opens-2026-with-flat-demand-and-rising-cost-pressure/); [Home News Now](https://homenewsnow.com/?p=72580); [Zelman](https://www.zelmanassociates.com/resources/zelman-insights/2026-02-(1)/furniture-demand-faces-the-elasticity-test,-but-ho); [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-research-reports/furniture-stores-industry/)
- **US import demand (A):** US furniture imports fell 11% in 2025 to $22.7bn (trade-data source). In Jan–Jul 2026, imports fell from about 38% to about 35% of apparent consumption, while domestic output edged up. — [Interior Daily](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9824913/vietnam-surges-ahead-as-china-s-grip-on-u-s-furniture-imports-weakens/); [Furnilytics](https://www.furnilytics.com/analytics/patterns-in-us-furniture-imports-supply-shifts-and-emerging-trade-pressures/)
- **Upholstery (CSIL global):** upholstery is one of the most labour-intensive furniture segments, so it is the most exposed to labour costs. About 20% of upholstery makers rate nearshoring as increasingly important. — [CSIL world upholstered furniture (via MarketResearch.com)](https://www.marketresearch.com/CSIL-Centre-for-Industrial-Studies-v1013/upholstered-furniture-38434519/)

### Inferences
- With flat volume demand in the EU and US, a new OEM supplier at Salone will mostly win by displacing incumbents (China, at-risk Vietnam suppliers, or high-cost EU capacity), not by riding market growth.
- Italian brands are under margin pressure: exports and volumes are down while energy and labour costs are high. That makes them more open to outsourcing a cost-competitive, high-quality upholstery line, especially for collections that do not need "Made in Italy" labelling (contract, private label, entry collections).

### Gaps
- I found no 2026–27 country-level forecasts for Germany, France, the Nordics or the UK; the full CSIL European Furniture Industry report would provide them.
- I found no Japan or Australia furniture demand forecast for 2026–27 (Yano Research / IBISWorld AU not retrieved).
- I found no upholstery-specific 2026 consumption growth rate.

## 2. Hospitality contract pipeline 2026–2028

### Takeaway
Hotel pipelines are at or near record highs in the Middle East, Asia-Pacific excluding China, and Europe. That makes hospitality FF&E the most dynamic demand pool, even though Saudi giga-project spending is being cut back.

### Cited Findings
- **Europe (Lodging Econometrics, Q2 2026, A):** 1,736 projects / 255,976 rooms, up about 3% YoY, with early planning at a record high. 773 projects / 116,172 rooms (45%) are under construction, and 332 projects are due to start within 12 months. The UK leads with 263 projects / 39,011 rooms. 112 hotels opened in H1 2026. Forecasts (F): 307 openings in 2026 (revised down from the 319 forecast in Q1) and 304 hotels / 43,099 rooms in 2028. — [LE Europe Q2 2026](https://lodgingeconometrics.com/europe-hotel-construction-pipeline-q2-2026/); [Rust Tourism News (Q1 forecast)](https://www.rustourismnews.com/2026/05/13/europe-hotel-development-pipeline-reaches-more-than-255000-rooms/)
- **Middle East (LE Q2 2026, A):** an all-time high of 724 projects / 178,003 rooms, up 11% by projects and 10% by rooms YoY. Saudi Arabia has 387 projects / 105,648 rooms (about 53% of projects and 59% of rooms), up 13% and 15% YoY. Riyadh has 106 projects / 21,666 rooms and Dubai 60 / 13,828. The UAE had 105 projects / 25,148 rooms at Q1 2026. 330 projects / 82,353 rooms (46%) are under construction. Openings forecast (F): 83 hotels / 15,149 rooms in 2026 and 91 / 22,875 in 2027. — [LE Middle East Q2 2026](https://lodgingeconometrics.com/middle-east-hotel-construction-pipeline-q2-2026/); [LE Q1 2026](https://lodgingeconometrics.com/middle-east-hotel-construction-pipeline-shows-positive-growth-in-q1-2026-reaches-a-record-high-717-projects/)
- **Saudi giga-project retrenchment (2026, A):** the FT reported in January 2026 that NEOM and The Line would be significantly scaled back and redesigned. AFP reported in February that Saudi Arabia is cutting its plan for 81 Red Sea luxury resorts by 2030. NEOM cancelled the Trojena ski-village contract in March. Zawya put cancelled or affected Q1 2026 works at potentially more than $5bn. Per MEED data cited by Skift, giga-project contract awards are about $10.6bn YTD, continuing a decline that began in 2024, but "hotel developers keep building". — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/saudi-arabia-downscales-flagship-red-sea-giga-project); [Euronews](https://euronews.com/culture/2026/01/26/neom-no-more-saudi-arabia-reduces-ambitious-plans-for-the-line-and-futuristic-megacity); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/projects/construction/pif-backed-neom-cancels-contracts-worth-billions-of-dollars-amid-regional-conflict-ainjqp28); [Skift](https://skift.com/?p=845839); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/giga-projects/2026/07/neom-rebrands-the-line-as-costs-and-complexity-bite/)
- **Asia-Pacific excluding China (LE, A):** a record 2,506 projects / 452,972 rooms at Q2 2026, with India at 1,033 projects (+36% YoY). Vietnam has 265 projects / 81,848 rooms, Japan 207, Indonesia 189 and Thailand 167. By Q3 2026 the region reached 2,684 projects / 473,560 rooms, with Japan at 225 projects. — [Hospitality Net Q2](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4133850/asia-pacific-excluding-china-hotel-construction-pipeline-reaches-record-highs-at-the-close-of-q2-2026-plus-new-2028-forecast); [Hospitality Net Q3](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4135289/record-early-planning-and-upscale-growth-drive-asia-pacific-excluding-china-pipeline-in-q3-2026)
- **China (LE, F):** more than 1,100 new hotel openings forecast for 2026, and conversions are at a record high. Conversions mean FF&E refurbishment demand. — [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4133819/china-1100-new-hotel-openings-forecast-for-2026-hotel-conversions-hit-record-high)
- **Global:** the LE global pipeline reached a record project count in Q2 2026, and the US leads the global pipeline. — [Lodging Magazine](https://lodgingmagazine.com/lodging-econometrics-global-hotel-construction-pipeline-reaches-record-high-project-count-in-q2-2026/)
- **FF&E procurement behaviour:** owners are budgeting 15–20% contingencies, rather than 5%, for tariff and supply-chain exposure. — [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/business-strategy/how-tariff-uncertainty-reshaping-project-budgets). EFM (an FF&E logistics firm) says most delays that affect openings happen in production and transit, not on site. — [EFM](https://efm.global/global-sourcing-local-deadlines-the-new-complexity-in-hotel-development/). SIS International (a commercial research firm) says owners are moving to two-region supplier panels and buying on total cost of ownership: lead time, replacement frequency and parts availability rather than unit price. — [SIS International](https://www.sisinternational.com/expertise/hotel-furniture-market-research/). Tariff mitigation in hospitality includes substituting tariff-advantaged sources for select FF&E. — [AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/us-tariffs-and-the-hospitality-industry-their-impact-and-what-watch-next); [Hospitality Design](https://hospitalitydesign.com/news/business-people/trump-tariffs-shake-up-hospitality-industry/)

### Inferences
- The Middle East remains the largest addressable hotel FF&E pool per project for a Thai upholsterer. The demand has moved from NEOM and Red Sea showpieces toward Riyadh and city hotels, Dubai, and sports-event infrastructure. Targets should be the brand-managed projects and procurement houses, not giga-project prestige.
- The UK leads the European pipeline, and the record early-planning stage implies 2027–2028 FF&E tenders. Salone in April 2027 is well timed to get on specification lists.
- Hotel buyers value schedule reliability and dual sourcing. A Thai supplier can present itself as the "second region" alongside Vietnam/China or EU suppliers.

### Gaps
- I found no hard FF&E spend figures (US$ per key or total market size for 2026–28) and no STR/CoStar 2026 data.
- I found no 2026 survey of procurement firms (e.g., Benjamin West, Purchasing Concepts, IHG/Marriott sourcing) on supplier criteria.
- I found no Australia hotel pipeline count.

## 3. US tariffs (late 2026), EU trade measures, EU–Thailand FTA

### Takeaway
For wooden-frame upholstered seating, the US rate is 25% under Section 232 regardless of Asian origin, so Thailand has no edge over Vietnam or China on that line. EU, Japan and UK origins enjoy capped rates (15% all-in, 10%). The general 232 rate is scheduled to rise to 30% on 1 Jan 2027 for countries without a deal. Products outside Section 232 face the new country-level Section 301 rates: 12.5% for Thailand, Vietnam and China, 10% for Malaysia and Indonesia. The EU–Thailand FTA is in its final stretch, with a target to conclude in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Section 232 (timber/lumber derivatives, A):** in effect from 14 Oct 2025, with 10% on softwood lumber, 25% on upholstered wooden furniture and 25% on kitchen cabinets and vanities. The upholstery scope is HTS 9401.61.4011, .4031, .6011 and .6031 (upholstered seats with wooden frames). — [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2025/october-14th/new-section-232-tariffs-imports-timber-lumber); [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2025q4/10-10-2025-client-advisory-cbp-issues-sec-232-tariff-guide-timber-furniture-cabinet-impo-oct-14-25/); [Mallory Alexander](https://www.mallorygroup.com/blog-posts/section-232-tariffs-on-lumber-upholstered-furniture-and-cabinets); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-White-House-Imposes-Section-232-Tariffs-on-Imports-of-Timber-Lumber-and-their-Derivative-Products)
- **Scheduled increase (A/F):** the rise from 25% to 30% on upholstered furniture, originally due 1 Jan 2026, was delayed to 1 Jan 2027 by the proclamation of 31 Dec 2025. The 30% rate does not apply to countries with which the US reaches an agreement. — [White House proclamation (amendments)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/?p=32531); [RVIA](https://www.rvia.org/news-insights/section-232-tariffs-wood-products-announced); [Ginger Control](https://gingercontrol.com/briefings/section-232-wood-products-tariffs-stay-at-25-through-7ec9df6a)
- **Country caps (A):** UK at 10%. EU and Japan at a combined Section 232 + MFN ceiling of 15%. Vietnam has no cap and pays the general 25% (Dimerco table, Feb 2026), and a previously announced 20% agreement with Vietnam had not been formalised. — [Shapiro](https://www.shapiro.com/?p=199639); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/?p=23544); [Dimerco tariff table, Feb 2026 (PDF)](https://lp.dimerco.com/hubfs/Content/US%20Tariff%20Table/US%20Tariff%20Table%20(Feb%2024%2c%202026).pdf)
- **IEEPA "reciprocal" tariffs struck down (A):** the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling ended the IEEPA tariffs, including Thailand's 19%. A 10% global Section 122 surcharge ran from 24 Feb to 24 Jul 2026; one law-firm note says 15%, but most sources say 10% throughout. — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/3202365/little-reprieve-for-thailand-after-us-tariff-ruling); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=330b8b26-8910-4ddf-88ca-fac577c04c52); [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-122-tariff-rates-2026)
- **New Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs (A, from 24 Jul 2026):** these cover about 60 countries. Thailand, Vietnam, China and Brazil pay 12.5% on top of MFN. Malaysia, Indonesia and 15 others pay a flat 10%. One Holland & Knight table row shows Vietnam at 13%, which conflicts with its own text. Section 232 products and USMCA-qualifying goods are exempt from this stack. For China, this sits on top of the legacy List 1–3 duties of 25%. Thailand is seeking a cut to 10% and more exemptions. A CIT ruling found Section 122 unlawful (under Federal Circuit review), and further 301 investigations could add tariffs. The American Action Forum described a proposed phased increase in 2027–28 that may not be final. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on); [Argents](https://argents.com/section-301-tariffs-2026-guide/); [Transatlantic Law](https://www.transatlanticlaw.com/content/thailand-new-12-5-us-section-301-tariff-takes-effect/); [American Action Forum](https://www.americanactionforum.org/research/the-new-section-301-tariff-regime/); [First Solar 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001274494/000127449426000170/fslr-20260630.htm)
- **Thai industry impact:** KResearch (Oct 2025, F) expected Thai furniture exports to rise about 9.3% in 2025 on front-loading and contract about 4.5% in 2026. It warned that upholstered wooden seating "may risk losing market share in the US", while cabinets and vanities stay competitive. — [Kasikorn Research](https://www.kasikornresearch.com/en/analysis/k-econ/business/Pages/FURNITURE-EXPORT-CIS3609-KR-2025-10-09.aspx). The Bangkok Post, via Market Research Thailand, reported that the 25% upholstery tariff is pushing exporters toward alternative markets. — [Market Research Thailand](https://marketresearchthailand.com/insights/article/thailand-furniture-export-industry-faces-new-pressures-and-fresh-openings-in-us-demand)
- **EU–Thailand FTA (A):** the 8th round was held 2–6 Feb 2026 and the 9th in Brussels in June, by which point 15 of 24 chapters were concluded. The 10th round was in Phuket from 28 Sep to 6 Oct 2026, and the Commission said "most chapters" are now provisionally closed (MLex, 12 Oct 2026). Market access for industrial goods, agriculture, procurement and services remain open. Both sides are targeting conclusion by end-2026, which means entry into force would come later, after ratification. — [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2536760); [Thai PRD](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/52/iid/504980); [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40072453); [Austrian Parliament (EU round 8 report)](https://www.parlament.gv.at/gegenstand/XXVIII/EU/60531)
- **EU regulatory hurdles:** Vietnamese exporters cite the EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation) and logistics costs as major hurdles in the EU market. — [Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1763962/viet-nam-s-wood-exports-reach-record-17-billion-in-value.html)

### Inferences
- **US competitiveness matrix (estimated, late 2026):**

  | Product | Thailand | Vietnam | China | EU, Japan | UK |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Wooden-frame upholstered seat, 9401.61 | 25% (Section 232) | 25% | 25%, plus possible legacy 301 (unverified) | 15% all-in | 10% |
  | Non-232 furniture, e.g. metal-frame upholstery 9401.71 or casegoods | MFN + 12.5% | MFN + 12.5% | MFN + 25% legacy + 12.5% | — | — |

  Malaysia and Indonesia pay MFN + 10% on non-232 goods.
- On its core US product, Thailand is level with Vietnam and only slightly better than China, and it is 10–15 points worse than Italian, EU and Japanese origin. For US-bound business, ASE's advantage has to come from quality, reliability and design co-creation, not tariffs. Metal-frame or other non-wood-frame constructions (outside 9401.61) may reduce exposure, but this needs a customs-classification check.
- The tariff picture strengthens the case for Salone: it points ASE toward EU-based brands and Middle East/European hospitality buyers, where US tariffs do not apply. A concluded EU–Thailand FTA would eventually remove EU MFN duties on Thai furniture. EU MFN duties on furniture are generally low, so the gain is modest, but it helps against Vietnam, which already has the EVFTA, from 2020.
- Watch 1 Jan 2027: if the 232 rate goes to 30% without a Thai deal, Thai upholstery to the US gets worse just before Salone.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the exact stacking of China's legacy 301 duties with Section 232 on 9401.61.
- I could not confirm whether Thailand has a 232 agreement or cap pending.
- I found no EU anti-dumping measures on Chinese furniture or furniture parts (not searched in depth; I am not aware of current ones on finished furniture, so treat as unverified).
- I did not retrieve exact EU MFN duty rates for HS 9401 from Thailand vs Vietnam (EVFTA).

## 4. "China+1" shift and Thailand's furniture exports

### Takeaway
China's share of US furniture imports has collapsed, from about 55% in 2018 to about 16% in 2025, and Vietnam captured most of it (about 42–46%). Thailand is a small, US-dependent player. Its furniture exports are roughly US$1.2–1.4bn and highly concentrated in the US, which makes diversification toward the EU, Middle East and Japan a strategic necessity.

### Cited Findings
- **US imports by origin (A, trade-data sources):** in 2025, total imports were $22.7bn (-11%). China fell 39% to $3.6bn, a 16% share, and Vietnam held a 42% share at $9.5bn. Over 2018–2024, China's share fell from 55% to 19% and Vietnam's rose from 17% to 43%. China and Vietnam together hold about 80% of US wood-frame upholstered seating, and Vietnam about 59% of upholstered wooden seats. — [Interior Daily](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9824913/vietnam-surges-ahead-as-china-s-grip-on-u-s-furniture-imports-weakens/); [GTAIC](https://gtaic.ai/trends/us-wooden-furniture-imports-shrinking-market-rewritten-supplier-map); [Furnilytics](https://www.furnilytics.com/analytics/patterns-in-us-furniture-imports-supply-shifts-and-emerging-trade-pressures/). Note: these are trade-data aggregators, not Census directly, and the figures do not fully reconcile.
- **Vietnam (A):** wood and wood-product exports were a record of about $17.2bn in 2025 (+6%). $9.46bn (55%) went to the US. Furniture was about $10.25bn by an industry estimate, versus $9.4bn for 11 months by the Ministry of Industry and Trade (unreconciled). Vietnam supplied 45.6% of US wooden-furniture imports in Jan–Nov 2025, up from 41.5%. The US share of Vietnam's wood exports fell from 54.6% to 50.4% in Jan 2026. Vietnam's H1 2026 furniture export growth is slowing. — [Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1763962/viet-nam-s-wood-exports-reach-record-17-billion-in-value.html); [VnEconomy](https://en.vneconomy.vn/wood-exports-hits-record-17-bln-despite-global-headwinds.htm); [Tuoi Tre](https://news.tuoitre.vn/vietnam-supplies-nearly-half-of-us-woodwork-imports-in-11-months-of-2025-10326022311543476.htm); [Furnilytics](https://www.furnilytics.com/analytics/vietnam-furniture-exports-in-h1-2026-slowing-growth/)
- **Thailand (mixed quality):** a market report gives furniture export value of US$1.38bn in 2023 (A). Per IndexBox estimates, not customs data, the US takes about 79% of Thai furniture and parts exports and Japan is second at about 12% in office furniture. 2025 US–Thailand trade in HS 94 was $1.23bn. A Thai Furniture Association figure cited by a trade blog shows +17.85% growth Jan 2025 to Jan 2026, which conflicts with KResearch's 2026 forecast of a 4.5% contraction. — [Research & Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5941169/thailand-furniture-industry-research-report); [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/thailand-furniture-and-parts-thereof-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/); [Market Research Thailand](https://marketresearchthailand.com/insights/article/thailand-furniture-export-industry-faces-new-pressures-and-fresh-openings-in-us-demand); [Thailand Woodworking](https://thailandwoodworking.com/industry-new/thailands-export-powerhouse-furniture-and-wood-products-surge-in-2026/); [KResearch](https://www.kasikornresearch.com/en/analysis/k-econ/business/Pages/FURNITURE-EXPORT-CIS3609-KR-2025-10-09.aspx)
- **Thai government support (2026):** DITP plans about 570 projects and 700 activities in 2026 (trade value target THB 140bn, about 300,000 businesses). These include a centralised sourcing database, a market-diversification Special Task Force, and targeted engagement with major US importers. DITP promotes Thai furniture at international fairs ("THINK THAILAND: NEXT LEVEL"). A Thai maker, Deesawat, urged exporters not to abandon the US (Bangkok Post, Oct 2025). Exporters report war-related cost increases. — [Khaosod English (sponsored)](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/sponsored/2026/04/21/ditp-launches-think-thailand-next-level-to-boost-global-exports/); [Thailand Business News](https://www.thailand-business-news.com/economics/287071-thai-exports-set-to-reach-bt142bn-in-2026); [Bangkok Post via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/Bangkok-Post/THAI-EXPORTERS-URGED-TO-FOCUS-ON-US); [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055089)

### Inferences
- The China+1 window in US upholstery has largely been captured by Vietnam. Thailand has no tariff edge over Vietnam on wood-frame upholstery. ASE's realistic China+1 pitch is "Vietnam alternative / dual source": an experienced 34-year OEM with stable governance, aimed at buyers concentrated in Vietnam who want to diversify risk (labour, transshipment scrutiny, capacity).
- Thai exporters' heavy US concentration (about 79% by the IndexBox estimate) makes Salone and EU, Middle East and Japan diversification consistent with DITP's own market-diversification agenda. ASE should check DITP subsidies for Salone 2027 booths and travel.

## 5. Buyer priorities (retailers, design brands seeking OEM/ODM, contract buyers); Italian outsourcing

### Takeaway
There is little hard 2026 survey data. The available evidence points to reliability and lead-time certainty, tariff-resilient dual sourcing, and total cost of ownership for contract buyers. European upholstery brands already offshore heavily, but mainly to Eastern Europe (Poland, Romania, Lithuania, Serbia), not Asia.

### Cited Findings
- About 40% of CSIL's Top 100 upholstered-furniture manufacturers produce outside their home country. European firms show the most delocalisation, with significant presence in Poland, Romania and Lithuania. — [CSIL via MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/CSIL-Centre-for-Industrial-Studies-v1013/upholstered-furniture-38434519/)
- Italy's upholstery imports come mainly from China, Romania and a fast-rising Serbia (CSIL 2026). — [CSIL Italy upholstery](https://www.worldfurnitureonline.com/report/the-upholstered-furniture-industry-in-italy/)
- Historically (CEPS/CSIL 2015, dated), Italian producers integrated production within the EU, building plants or partnering locally, mostly in upholstered and seating. — [CEPS/CSIL 2015 (PDF)](https://www.ceps.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Final%20report_en.pdf)
- About 20% of upholstery makers see nearshoring as increasingly important (CSIL). — [CSIL via MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/CSIL-Centre-for-Industrial-Studies-v1013/upholstered-furniture-38434519/)
- Contract and hotel buyers: 15–20% budget contingencies for tariffs ([Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/business-strategy/how-tariff-uncertainty-reshaping-project-budgets)); production and transit as the main delay source ([EFM](https://efm.global/global-sourcing-local-deadlines-the-new-complexity-in-hotel-development/)); TCO, lead time and parts availability over unit price, plus two-region supplier panels ([SIS International](https://www.sisinternational.com/expertise/hotel-furniture-market-research/)).
- US retail: retailers are running tighter inventories and more discounting ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-research-reports/furniture-stores-industry/)). Premium upholstery, customisation and showroom-sold high-value upholstery are lifting revenue per item ([Freedonia](https://www.freedoniagroup.com/Household-Furniture-United-States-8911568/)).
- EU compliance: the EUDR is cited as a key hurdle for Asian wood-product exporters to the EU. — [Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1763962/viet-nam-s-wood-exports-reach-record-17-billion-in-value.html)

### Inferences
- Italian and European brands that buy upholstery abroad mostly choose Eastern Europe for proximity: short lead times and small runs. To win them, ASE would need to offset distance with consolidated-container programmes, EU stock or finishing options, documented EUDR traceability for wood frames, and design co-creation capability. This fits ASE's "co-creation partner, not supplier" positioning.
- Tighter retailer inventories imply demand for smaller MOQs and replenishment. Long ocean lead times from Thailand (about 4–6 weeks of transit to the EU, my estimate, not sourced) are a structural disadvantage against Poland, Romania and Serbia in fast retail. Contract and hospitality, being project-based and scheduled, is the better fit.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 quantitative survey ranking buyer criteria (lead time, MOQ, price, sustainability) for EU/US retailers or brands.
- I found no named Italian brands outsourcing upholstery to Asia or Thailand.
- I found no data on Salone buyer composition (FLA/Salone visitor statistics were not retrieved).

## 6. Price positioning: Thai vs Italian, Portuguese, Polish, Vietnamese, Chinese upholstery

### Takeaway
Hard comparative cost data is scarce and dated. Thailand's wages are similar to or above Vietnam's (Thai minimum about US$249/month vs Vietnamese factory wages of about US$250–450/month by vendor claims). Poland and Portugal labour costs (2019) run at about €12–14k per employee per year. Thai upholstery plausibly sits mid-market: above Vietnam and China on cost and craft, below EU producers.

### Cited Findings
- Vietnam factory wages are about $250–450/month (sourcing-firm claim). — [MoveToAsia](https://movetoasia.com/en/vietnam/sourcing-manufacturing/furniture/cost/)
- Thailand's average minimum wage is about US$248.94/month (undated, not upholstery-specific). — search summary from vendor and sourcing pages; the primary source was not verified.
- Furniture-manufacturing labour cost per employee FTE (2019): Portugal €14.2k, Poland €11.9k. — [Portugal labour cost series](https://d3fqfiq2w1ez7u.cloudfront.net/nmx/timeseries/portugal-furniture-manufacturing-labour-cost-per-employee-fte); [Ranking](https://d3fqfiq2w1ez7u.cloudfront.net/nmx/ranking/furniture-manufacturing-labour-cost-per-employee-fte)
- US furniture buyers are eyeing Cambodia as Vietnam's wages rise. — [Air Freight News](https://airfreight.news/articles/full/u.s-furniture-industry-eyes-cambodia-as-vietnamas-wages-rise)

### Inferences
- Since ASE cannot win on labour cost against Vietnam or Cambodia, and cannot win on proximity against Poland, Romania or Portugal, its defensible slot is the craft-quality, premium mid-market: Italian-level finish at a meaningful discount, especially for hospitality and contract programmes and design brands' secondary lines. This is consistent with the brand positioning (premium, crafted, calm).
- Combined with Section 3, this suggests the following priority order for Salone targeting. (a) Middle East and European hospitality FF&E procurement and contract specifiers. (b) EU and UK design brands seeking a non-China upholstery partner for contract and secondary collections. (c) Japanese buyers, an existing relationship with no US-tariff exposure. (d) US buyers mainly as Vietnam/China dual-source accounts, recognising the tariff parity.

### Gaps
- I found no unit-price or FOB benchmarks per seat or sofa by origin (e.g., CSIL price-range data).
- I found no 2024–26 Eurostat labour costs for Poland, Portugal or Romania furniture.
- I found no upholstery-specific Thai wage or productivity data.
