# Regulation & Compliance Landscape — Upholstered Furniture (Leather, Textiles, Foam, Wood Frames) from Thailand to EU / UK / US / Middle East (status as of 9 Nov 2026, outlook 2027–2028)

> Method note: All findings come from web search result summaries (WebSearch). Full-page fetches of two law-firm pages (hlc.com, carbonchaincode.com) were blocked by the session network policy, so several items rely on secondary/vendor summaries. ## 1. EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) — leather, wood, rubberwood

### Takeaway
EUDR now applies from **30 Dec 2026** (large/medium operators) and **30 Jun 2027** (micro/small), and **bovine leather was removed from scope** by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102 (in force 18 Sep 2026). **Wooden furniture (9403) and wooden/wood-frame seats (9401) stay in scope**, so an ASE sofa on a wooden (incl. rubberwood) frame is an EUDR product; leather upholstery itself is no longer the EUDR issue. Thailand is classified **low risk** (simplified due diligence), but geolocation of the timber's plots of origin is still required.

### Cited Findings
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 (Dec 2025 amendment) moved application to **30 December 2026**; Commission review of 4 May 2026 kept the dates; no further postponement proposed — [EY Tax News](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0237-eu-deforestation-regulation-application-postponed-to-30-december-2026); [EU Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation); [Mayer Brown, Feb 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/eu-regulation-on-deforestation-free-products-eudr-what-lies-ahead-in-2026)
- Micro and small operators (outside the timber sector, per one summary) have a deferred date of **30 June 2027** — [QIMA](https://www.qima.com/blog/sustainability/eudr-deadline-2026); [IntegrityNext](https://www.integritynext.com/resources/blog/article/eudr-delay-2025-2026-what-it-means-for-supply-chains-and-procurement). Uncertainty: sources conflict on whether micro/small *timber* operators get the 2027 date — verify in Reg. 2025/2650.
- The Commission's May 2026 simplification package did not reopen the EUDR text; it works through a delegated act changing product scope — [HLC summary via search](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-deforestation-regulation-commission-publishes-simplification-package-ahead-of-december-2026); [Global ELR, May 2026](https://www.globalelr.com/2026/05/european-commission-releases-new-eu-deforestation-regulation-measures/)
- **Leather removed**: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) **2026/2102** adopted 13 Jul 2026, published OJ 17 Sep 2026, in force **18 Sep 2026**; deletes HS 4101, 4104, 4107 (cattle hides, skins, leather) from Annex I. Live cattle and beef remain in scope. Leather to be reconsidered in the general scope review due **2030** — [Euroleather press release](https://euroleather.com/news/latest/1365-press-release-leather-out-of-the-eudr-european-commission-adopts-delegated-act); [World Footwear](https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/european-commission-formally-removes-leather-from-eudr-scope/11640.html); [Coolset (updated Sep 2026)](https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-product-scope-changes-are-official-what-the-july-2026-delegated-regulation-means-for-compliance); [Coolx](https://coolx.earth/en/blog/eudr-annex-i-delegated-regulation-2026-2102)
- Same act removes "other articles of vulcanised rubber" (ex 4016) and aircraft/motor-vehicle seats; **natural rubber (4001) and tyres stay in**; soluble coffee, some palm derivatives added from 30 Dec 2027 — [Zeelandia Consulting](https://zeelandiaconsulting.com/en/insights/eudr-product-scope-2026-2102/); [carbmee](https://www.carbmee.com/knowledge-insights/blog-article/eudr-automotive-leather-rubber); [Coolx](https://coolx.earth/en/blog/eudr-annex-i-delegated-regulation-2026-2102)
- **Furniture after 2026/2102**: heading 9401 now limited to six subheadings for **seats of wood or with a wooden frame** (e.g., 9401 31, 41, 61, 69, 80, 91 90 as "ex" codes); metal-frame seats (9401 71/79) not listed; **wooden furniture 9403 not removed** — [iCustoms Annex I list](https://www.icustoms.ai/blogs/eudr-hs-cn-codes/); [Polygon-one furniture importers](https://polygon-one.com/en/eudr-furniture-importers); [APA Engineering](https://apaengineering.com/compliance-news/eudr-scope-revised-2026/). Conflict: some sources describe the 9401 entry as "removed", others as "narrowed" — vendor blogs; verify on EUR-Lex.
- Used/second-hand products excluded from the "ex" codes — [ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/eu-deforestation-regulation-products/)
- **Thailand = low risk** in the EU country benchmarking list (22 May 2025); Thai officials say this means simplified due diligence and ~1% inspection rate (government claim, not verified against text) — [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/40050892); [The Nation on rubber](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40069064)
- Benchmarking is reviewed (2026 review) and countries can move between tiers — [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/2-eudr-country-risk-classification-review-2026-what-changes-when-the-benchmarks-are-updated)
- EU-funded studies assessed Thailand's existing standards/tools for EUDR compliance and wood/palm sector readiness — [EEAS Study 1 summary](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025/documents/Executive%20Summary%20EUDR%20Study%201%20Assessment%20of%20Thailand%E2%80%99s%20Existing%20Standards%20and%20Tools%20for%20EUDR%20Compliance.pdf); [EEAS Study 3](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025/documents/Executive%20Summary%20EUDR%20Study%203%20Cross-Sectoral%20Analysis%20and%20Stakeholder%20Engagement%20for%20EUDR%20Implementation%20in%20Thailand%E2%80%99s%20Palm%20Oil%20and%20Wood%20Sectors.pdf); Thai Latex Association news — [TLA](https://tla-latex.org/EN/news_detail.php?id=1050)
- Older (2023) analysis: >90% of Thai processed-wood exports go to China, so EUDR effects on Thai wood were seen as mostly indirect — [Krungsri Research](https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/research-intelligence/eudr-2023); vendor TraceX labels Thai rubber "moderate risk", contradicting official low-risk tier — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/?p=24674)
- SGS Thailand notes FSC/PEFC as supporting (not replacing) EUDR due diligence — [SGS Thailand](https://www.sgs.com/en-th/news/2026/02/understanding-the-eu-deforestation-regulation-and-its-impact-on-businesses)

### Inferences
- What EU buyers will ask at Salone (Apr 2027, i.e. ~4 months after application): "Is the frame wood? Can you give me geolocation of the plantation/forest plots and a due-diligence statement reference?" For rubberwood frames this means plot-level coordinates of the rubber plantations the sawn timber came from — Thai rubberwood comes from smallholder plantations, so traceability through sawmills is the hard part. A maker who already holds that data is selling "zero customs friction" to the importer (the EU importer is the "operator" who carries the liability).
- Leather removal removes an expected cost for Thai tanneries/leather sourcing, but buyers may still ask for origin of hides for ESG reasons (LWG traceability) — EUDR is no longer the lever; LWG/brand ESG is.
- Low-risk status lowers the importer's burden but does not remove the geolocation requirement (standard practice under EUDR; confirm Art. 13 simplified DD scope).
- Selling point: "EUDR-ready wooden frames with plot-level geolocation, FSC/PEFC where possible" vs low-cost competitors who cannot trace mixed rubberwood lots.

### Gaps
- Exact text of Art. 13 simplified due diligence for low-risk countries (whether geolocation still mandatory) — could not fetch EUR-Lex.
- Whether micro/small *timber-sector* operators also get 30 Jun 2027 — sources conflict.
- No quantified data found on share of Thai rubberwood furniture exports to EU or on smallholder traceability cost.
- Whether upholstered seats with wooden *internal* frames classified under 9401 61 are captured in every case — needs customs classification check per SKU.

## 2. ESPR, Digital Product Passport, destruction ban, durability; Green Claims / ECGT

### Takeaway
Furniture (incl. mattresses) is an ESPR **priority group**, but the furniture delegated act is only expected **~2028**, so mandatory furniture DPP/durability rules are unlikely before **~2029–2030**. The unsold-goods destruction ban currently covers **apparel and footwear only** (large firms from 19 Jul 2026). The binding claims rule today is the **Empowering Consumers (ECGT) Directive, applying from 27 Sep 2026**; the separate **Green Claims Directive is being withdrawn** (2027 Commission work programme, 4 Nov 2026).

### Cited Findings
- First ESPR Working Plan 2025–2030 adopted **16 Apr 2025**; furniture (incl. mattresses) is a priority product group — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/zh-hans/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/european-commission-releases-list-of-priority-products); [One Click LCA](https://oneclicklca.com/en-gb/resources/articles/first-espr-working-plan); [Open Access Government](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/the-2025-2030-espr-plan-to-promote-sustainable-products/192186/)
- Furniture delegated act expected **2028**, mattresses 2029; with ≥18-month transition, first requirements ~**2029–2030** — [Furniture News, Dec 2025](https://furniturenews.net/resources/the-esprs-dpp-countdown); [Furniture Production, Mar 2026](https://furnitureproduction.net/resources/what-the-eus-espr-means-for-uk-furniture-industry-in-three-key-milestones); [Projet Celsius FAQ](https://projetcelsius.com/en/faq/dpp-espr-affichage-env/quand-l-ameublement-sera-t-il-concerne-par-l-espr/). Conflict: a tracker site gives adoption 2026–27 and DPP 2028–29 but cites an outdated plan — [esprregistry](https://esprregistry.pages.dev/furniture-delegated-act-tracker) (low weight).
- Furniture DPP data set not yet adopted; the delegated act will set scope, data requirements (expected to include durability, repairability, material composition, substances of concern) — [Renoon furniture DPP](https://www.renoon.com/digital-product-passport-furniture); [Circularise ESPR guide](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/a-guide-to-the-ecodesign-for-sustainable-products-regulation-espr)
- **Destruction ban**: applies to unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear for **large companies from 19 Jul 2026**; medium companies **2030**; delegated act (Feb 2026) sets ten derogations; Commission can extend via Annex VII amendments; disclosure of discarded unsold goods reportedly from **Feb 2027** (single source) — [European Commission, 9 Feb 2026](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-stop-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-2026-02-09_en); [Cooley, May 2026](https://products.cooley.com/2026/05/07/deep-dive-eu-finalises-new-requirements-for-unsold-consumer-products-under-espr/); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102miiy/eu-espr-commission-adopts-final-acts-on-unsold-consumer-product-destruction-and); [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/insights/espr-the-ban-on-destruction-of-unsold-goods/)
- **Green Claims Directive**: Commission's 2027 work programme (4 Nov 2026) lists it for withdrawal; remains formally pending until withdrawal procedure completes; first withdrawal signal June 2025 — [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2546190); [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/esg-update-green-claims-directive-set-to-be-withdrawn-but-anti-greenwashing-rules-remain/); [Mintz, Mar 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2026-03-16-eu-commission-abandons-anti-greenwashing-law); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-announces-intention-to-withdraw-eu-green-claims-directive-proposal)
- **ECGT Directive** (Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition) applies from **27 Sep 2026**; bans generic/unsubstantiated environmental claims (e.g., "eco", "green", "sustainable" without recognised excellent performance), and climate-neutral claims based on offsetting; unaffected by Green Claims withdrawal — [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/esg-update-green-claims-directive-set-to-be-withdrawn-but-anti-greenwashing-rules-remain/); [Packaging Europe](https://www.packagingeurope.com/green-claims-directive-grinds-to-a-halt-as-eu-commission-withdraws-proposal/)
- Without the Green Claims Directive, there is no harmonised third-party pre-verification of claims — [Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/esg-update-green-claims-directive-set-to-be-withdrawn-but-anti-greenwashing-rules-remain/)

### Inferences
- At Salone 2027, EU retailers selling to consumers are already under ECGT: they will refuse to repeat words like "sustainable/eco-friendly" on ASE pieces unless backed by a recognised certification/ecolabel or specific, verifiable data. ASE copy should use specific, provable statements (e.g., "FSC-certified frame", "LWG Gold leather", "replaceable cushion cores") — consistent with the brand rule "no sustainability claims without real process".
- DPP for furniture is a 2029+ obligation, but buyers are already asking for DPP-ready data (bill of materials, material origin, repair info). Offering a voluntary QR "product passport" in 2027 is a differentiator, not a compliance must.
- Repairability/durability (replaceable covers, separable foam, mechanical fixings vs glue) is the likely content of the furniture act — designing for it now is a credible "Ease of Touch/Ease for the Earth" story.

### Gaps
- Official Commission page for furniture delegated act / JRC preparatory study status not fetched.
- No evidence found of a proposal to extend the destruction ban to furniture.
- ECGT transposition variation by member state (e.g., Italy, France) not researched.

## 3. Chemicals and EPR (REACH: PFAS, flame retardants, Cr VI, formaldehyde; furniture & textile EPR)

### Takeaway
Two hard limits apply now: **Cr VI ≤3 mg/kg in skin-contact leather** (since 2015) and **formaldehyde emission ≤0.062 mg/m³ for furniture and wood-based articles (from 6 Aug 2026)**. The **universal PFAS restriction** is still a proposal (RAC opinion March 2026; SEAC final opinion expected end-2026; Commission proposal + REACH Committee vote afterwards) — upholstery/leather/textile stain-repellent finishes are a named sector. Textile EPR will be national schemes by **Apr 2028**.

### Cited Findings
- **Chromium VI**: Reg. (EU) No 301/2014 amended REACH Annex XVII entry 47; leather articles (and articles with leather parts) in contact with skin ≤ **3 mg/kg** Cr VI (dry weight); applies since **1 May 2015**; test method EN ISO 17075 — [EUR-Lex 2014/301](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/301/oj); [SATRA](https://satra.com/bulletin/article.php?id=1263); [legislation.gov.uk (retained in UK)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/301)
- **Formaldehyde**: Reg. (EU) 2023/1464 added entry 77 to Annex XVII; emission limit **0.062 mg/m³ for wood-based articles and furniture** (0.080 mg/m³ other articles); applies from **6 Aug 2026** (vehicles 2027); test per Appendix 14; roughly half the old E1 threshold (0.124 mg/m³), so E1 panels are not automatically compliant — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-6-2023/eu-reach-annex-xvii-restriction-on-formaldehyde-emission-was-published); [Fraunhofer WKI](https://wki.fraunhofer.de/en/departments/qa/profile/news/certification-reach.html); [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/formaldehyde-testing-and-regulations-in-the-eu/); [HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/news/eu-formaldehyde-restrictions/). Transitional provisions for goods already on the market are described inconsistently — check text.
- **PFAS universal restriction**: RAC adopted final opinion **2 Mar 2026**; SEAC draft opinion agreed 10 Mar, published 26 Mar 2026 with 60-day consultation (to 25 May 2026); SEAC final opinion expected **by end-2026**; then Commission proposal and REACH Committee vote. Sectors include "textiles, upholstery, leather, apparel and carpets"; SEAC could not conclude on derogations for eight sectors — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/support-for-eu-wide-pfas-restriction-with-targeted-derogations-what-happens-next); [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/echa-adopts-opinion-on-proposed-universal-pfas-restriction); [ChemLinked](https://chemical.chemlinked.com/news/chemical-news/echa-risk-assessment-committee-adopts-opinion-on-universal-pfas-restriction); [Freyr](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/consumer-industry-news/european-union-advances-pfas-restriction-proposal-to-reduce-environmental-and-human-health-risks). Conflict: Eurofins (Apr 2026) said RAC deadline extended to June 2026 and SEAC final Sept 2026 — [Eurofins](https://www.eurofins.com/en/consumer-product-testing/news/chemicals-monthly-bulletin-april-2026/) (garbled; low weight).
- **Textile EPR**: revised Waste Framework Directive entered into force mid-Oct 2025; transposition by **17 Jun 2027**; national textile & footwear EPR schemes by **17 Apr 2028**; micro-enterprises get +1 year; scope includes household linens, curtains, potentially mattresses (member-state choice) — [EUWID](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/waste-framework-directive-published-textile-epr-rules-take-effect-in-april-2028-260925/); [European Commission](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/revised-waste-framework-directive-enters-force-2025-10-16_de); [B&D Law](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/eu-targets-textile-waste-with-waste-framework-directive-amendments/)
- UK textile/leather skin-sensitiser restriction proposals noted in REACH pipeline (skin-sensitising substances in textiles and leather) — [TÜV SÜD 2019](https://www.tuvsud.com/ja-jp/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-9-2019/eu-reach-annex-xvii-restriction-on-skin-sensitising-substances-in-textiles-and-leather-was-proposed) (status of adoption not verified).

### Inferences
- Upholstery fabrics with fluorinated stain-repellent finishes are the main PFAS exposure; a PFAS-free fabric/leather finish spec is a low-cost differentiator to adopt before the restriction (likely adopted no earlier than 2027–2028, with transition periods) — timing inferred, not sourced.
- Formaldehyde: Thai MDF/particleboard/plywood used in bases and frames needs test reports to the new Annex XVII method; many low-cost panel suppliers only state E1 — a test certificate is a real buyer question in 2027.
- The importer/"producer" in France must join furniture EPR (Ecomaison) and pay eco-contributions; a Thai maker can help by supplying material breakdowns per SKU.

## 4. Flammability — UK, US, contract/hospitality, Middle East

### Takeaway
**UK**: the 1988 Furniture & Furnishings (Fire)(Safety) Regulations still apply; reform consultation (smoulder-based approach, less reliance on flame-retardant chemicals, possible removal of open-flame test) ran **31 Mar–23 Jun 2026**; no final rules found. **US**: TB117-2013 is federal law (16 CFR 1640) since **25 Jun 2021**, with mandatory label. Middle East: Saudi SASO/SABER conformity required; furniture-specific flammability mandates unclear.

### Cited Findings
- UK DBT/OPSS consultation "fire safety of domestic upholstered furniture" launched **31 Mar 2026**, closed **23 Jun 2026**; under Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025; proposes replacing 1988 regs with smoulder-testing-based rules, reducing chemical flame retardants; second-hand and re-upholstery to move under General Product Safety Regulations — [gov.uk consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/product-regulation-fire-safety-of-domestic-upholstered-furniture); [FIRA](https://www.fira.co.uk/news/article/consultation-launched-fire-safety-of-domestic-upholstered-furniture); [Furniture Makers' Company](https://furnituremakers.org.uk/news/government-launches-consultation-on-fire-safety-of-upholstered-furniture/); [FPA](https://www.thefpa.co.uk/fire-and-risk-management-journal/news/furniture-fire-safety-consultation-open-for-comment)
- Reported proposal includes removing the open-flame ignition test; industry views mixed — [Interior Daily](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9825409/uk-seeks-industry-input-on-major-upholstered-furniture-fire-safety-overhaul); [Furniture News (Jun–Jul 2026 commentaries)](https://www.furniturenews.net/resources/claire-kelly-on-flammability-regulation-reform)
- Earlier: Jan 2025 policy paper; concerns over SME burden prompted further review — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/knowledge-hub/technical-updates/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/uk-policy-paper-on-the-furniture-and-furnishings-fire-safety-regulations-published); [gov.uk news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/update-to-furniture-fire-safety-review)
- No consultation response or draft SI found as of Nov 2026 (search returned none).
- **US**: COVID-19 Regulatory Relief and Work From Home Safety Act (2020) made TB117-2013 a federal standard under the Flammable Fabrics Act; CPSC codified at **16 CFR part 1640**; applies to upholstered furniture manufactured/imported/reupholstered on or after **25 Jun 2021**; June 2013 or Jan 2019 TB117 versions accepted; permanent label "Complies with U.S. CPSC requirements for upholstered furniture flammability" — [eCFR 16 CFR 1640](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-1640); [Federal Register 9 Apr 2021](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2021-04-09/html/2021-06977.htm); [CPSC FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Upholstered-Furniture); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2021/07/cpsc-flammability-upholstered-furniture-effective/)
- **Saudi Arabia**: SABER platform issues Product Certificate/Type Approval and Shipment Certificate, mandatory for market entry; part of SALEEM product safety programme; SASO standard on ignitability of mattresses/upholstered bed bases (match-flame equivalent) and 2026 draft on PU foam moulding flammability exist — [TÜV SÜD SASO/SABER](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-ae/services/product-certification/saso-saber-product-certification-in-saudi-arabia); [HQTS SABER guide](https://www.hqts.com/?p=240092); [SASO DS 33926](https://saso.gov.sa/ar/Laws-And-Regulations/public_multimedia/Documents/SASO-DS-33926.pdf); [SGS Saudi furniture testing](https://www.sgs.com/en-sa/services/furniture-testing)
- UAE: Emirates Safety Laboratory performs fire testing for products under UAE civil defence regime — [International Fire & Safety Journal](https://internationalfireandsafetyjournal.com/safeguarding-public-safety-with-emirates-safety-laboratory/) (furniture-specific mandates not confirmed).

### Inferences
- UK: the 1988 rules (cigarette, match, and Schedule 5 cover tests; filling tests) are what UK buyers check in 2027; reform is unlikely to apply before 2028 given consultation timing (inference). A maker able to pass both current UK rules and EN 1021-1/-2 *without* halogenated FRs is positioned for the reformed regime.
- US: TB117-2013 is smoulder-only, so it is achievable without FR chemicals; Thai makers already exporting to the US should have test reports + label.
- Contract/hospitality: buyers typically specify BS 5852 Crib 5 (UK hotels/public), EN 1021-1/2 (EU, often plus national rules such as Italy's Class 1 IM or French M-ratings), and CAL TB 133 / NFPA 260 (US public occupancy). This is background knowledge — not verified in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No sourced confirmation of current BS 5852 Crib 5, EN 1021, CAL 133 specification practice (search returned nothing citable).
- Italy (host market) contract rules — Ministerial Decree classes (Classe 1 IM) for public spaces — not researched.
- UAE / Dubai Civil Defence furniture flammability requirements — no reliable source found.
- Whether SASO mandates specific upholstered furniture flammability tests under SABER — unclear.

## 5. Certifications buyers value — cost/time for a mid-size Thai factory

### Takeaway
Certifications most relevant to ASE's materials: **FSC/PEFC chain of custody** (wood/rubberwood, also supports EUDR evidence), **LWG** (leather — tannery-level; ASE as buyer should source LWG-rated leather), **OEKO-TEX** (textiles), **CertiPUR** (foam), and optionally EU Ecolabel/GREENGUARD for contract. Specific cost figures were not found; they are quote-based.

### Cited Findings
- LWG does not charge for audits; independent third-party auditors perform them; LWG charges an admin fee (one undated LWG page cited £300); auditor fees depend on scope/sites — [LWG auditing](https://www.leatherworkinggroup.com/auditing); [LWG get certified](https://www.leatherworkinggroup.com/get-involved/get-certified/); [LWG certification](https://leatherworkinggroup.com/certification)
- LWG has four standards across the chain (traders, leather manufacturers, subcontractors, finished leather traders) — [LWG understanding certification](https://www.leatherworkinggroup.com/certification/understanding-certification/); [LWG Leather Manufacturer Standard](https://www.leatherworkinggroup.com/certification/leather-manufacturer-standard/)
- Bangladesh study found LWG certification cost a barrier and asked for state support; analysts linked lax compliance to 30–40% lower leather goods export earnings — [Financial Express BD](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/moi-should-place-budgetary-bid-to-help-tanneries-1677693505); [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/853066)
- FSC CoC cost and duration vary case by case; quotes from accredited certification bodies; group certification reduces cost for small firms (UK: <15 employees, or <25 with turnover <$1m); group members spend ~10–15 hours/year — [FSC UK CoC FAQ](https://uk.fsc.org/chain-of-custody-certification/chain-of-custody-faqs); [FSC UK group schemes](https://uk.fsc.org/chain-of-custody-certification/chain-of-custody-group-schemes); [FSC GA 2025 poster](https://ga.fsc.org/es/ga-2025-poster-ulf-sonntag-et-al)

### Inferences
- A mid-size Thai factory would likely not qualify for FSC group schemes (size thresholds) and would need individual CoC certification — inferred from UK thresholds; Thai group schemes may differ.
- LWG applies to tanneries/traders; for ASE (a furniture maker buying leather, or using family leather operation) the buyer question will be "is your leather from an LWG-rated tannery (Gold/Silver)?" If ASE's leather supply is in-house tanning, LWG audit becomes directly relevant.
- Hierarchy of buyer value (inference): EUDR geolocation data (mandatory) > formaldehyde/Cr VI test reports (mandatory) > FSC/PEFC + LWG (most-asked voluntary) > OEKO-TEX/CertiPUR > EPD > EU Ecolabel / GREENGUARD / Cradle to Cradle (premium, contract-led).

### Gaps

## 6. Contract / hospitality procurement — ESG reporting, Scope 3, EPDs

### Takeaway
CSRD has been narrowed by the Omnibus I directive (2026), shrinking the number of buyers legally obliged to report and capping what they can demand from small suppliers (VSME level), but large hotel groups and specifiers still ask for product carbon data, with **verified EPDs** increasingly a tender qualification.

### Cited Findings
- Omnibus I (Directive (EU) 2026/470) narrows CSRD scope; one source cites thresholds >1,000 employees and €450m turnover (Feb 2026 Council statement); revised ESRS adopted July 2026 — [Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/insights/publications/esg-disclosure-and-corporate-due-diligence-obligations-under-the-omnibus-reform/); [printplast (vendor, internally inconsistent)](https://www.printplast.com/csrd-2026-hotels-sustainable-procurement/)
- Value-chain cap: reporting companies expected to limit data requests to suppliers <1,000 employees to the VSME standard — [EcoVadis](https://ecovadis.com/es/blog/value-chain-action-reporting-after-omnibus/); [Sunhat](https://www.getsunhat.com/blog/esrs-value-chain)
- Verified EPD described as most credible format for Scope 3 Category 1 data and increasingly a tender condition (vendor source) — [Nexio Projects](https://nexioprojects.com/environmental-product-declarations-why-your-supply-chain-needs-one-in-2026/)
- Hospitality Alliance for Responsible Procurement (HARP) treats verified product carbon footprint data as supplier advantage — [EcoVadis HARP](https://sector.ecovadis.com/the-hospitality-alliance-for-responsible-procurement-initiative-harp/accelerating-decarbonization-across-the-hospitality-supply-chain/); hospitality procurement shifting from ESG messaging to mandatory disclosure — [Hospitalitynet](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4131127/from-esg-messaging-to-mandatory-disclosure-the-growing-role-of-hospitality-procurement); [Hospitalitynet 2](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4133163/sustainable-procurement-in-hospitality-policy-shifts-guest-expectations-and-the-supplier-response)

### Inferences
- For ASE PROJECTS (contract/hospitality), an EPD (EN 15804 / furniture PCR) for 2–3 hero products plus a per-SKU material/origin sheet would answer most specifier requests; this fits the "co-creation partner, not supplier" positioning (sharing data openly).
- Regulatory pressure is falling (Omnibus), but commercial pressure from global hotel groups and architects (LEED/BREEAM/WELL projects) continues — inference.

### Gaps
- No primary data on how many hotel groups mandate EPDs for loose furniture; no BIFMA/level or furniture PCR specifics found.
- Final Omnibus I thresholds and phase-in dates should be verified on EUR-Lex (sources conflict).
- US contract market (BIFMA LEVEL, GSA) requirements not researched.
