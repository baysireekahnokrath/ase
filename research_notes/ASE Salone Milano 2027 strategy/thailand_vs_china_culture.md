# Thailand vs China: Craft Differentiation and the Cultural Moment for ASE at Milan Design Week 2027

> Research method note (2026-11-09): this pass relied on web search results. Direct page fetches were blocked by the session network for dezeen.com, khaosodenglish.com and thestar.com.my, so findings from those outlets come from search snippets, not full articles. Many sources are vendor, PR or government-linked. Each limitation is flagged inline. ## Q1. Where Chinese furniture manufacturing leads, and where buyers are wary

### Takeaway
China leads on scale, cluster density, automation equipment and small-MOQ trading access. Its exposure is now mainly policy and trust: US tariffs cut US imports of Chinese furniture by about 31% in 2025, transshipment scrutiny is rising, and copy-culture and batch-consistency risks are well known. I found no buyer survey that quantifies IP or UFLPA concerns for furniture specifically. US Section 232 upholstery tariffs apply to Thailand as well, so price is not where Thailand wins.

### Cited Findings
**China's strengths**
- Foshan/Shunde cluster scale. Vendor figures conflict: "over 1,200 furniture factories" in Longjiang township alone; "over 3,000" across Shunde, Lecong and Gaoming; "over 5,000 solid wood and panel furniture enterprises" with output above RMB 100bn; Lecong wholesale space above 5 million m². All are vendor or sourcing-guide claims, not official statistics — [CC Furniture](https://chiuchiufurniture.com/blogs/chiu-blog/sourcing-furniture-from-foshan-factories-guide); [CIFF Click2Connect](https://click2connect.ciff-gz.com/blogs/69ec6e63a1e2d46b1a1200be); [Dev.to sourcing agent](https://dev.to/china-sourcing-agents/foshan-sourcing-agent-furniture-ceramics-home-appliances-24b5)
- Foshan is called the centre of China's upholstered-furniture exports, with "over 40%" of national sofa exports. No data source is given (UNVERIFIED) — [Alibaba sourcing page](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/sofa-factory-com)
- Automation is local. Shunde machinery makers (e.g. SYUTECH, 80% export ratio) supply panel-furniture automation and vacuum pressing. I found no independent figure for how widely automation is adopted — [HTNXT/SYUTECH](https://www.htnxt.com/page/SYUTECH:-A-Rising-Force-in-Woodworking-Machinery-from-the-Heart-of-Foshan.html)
- Small-batch access. Suppliers advertise MOQs as low as 2 pieces through wholesalers and trading companies — [China Importal](https://www.chinaimportal.com/?p=3683)
- Chinese design brands at Milan. Shanghai-based Stellar Works calls itself "the first global furniture design brand from Asia" and won Wallpaper* Best New Brand in 2013 — [Indesignlive](https://www.indesignlive.com/singapore/products/stellar-works-east-west-dialogue); [Openers 2013](https://openers.jp/en/design/design_features/17045). At MDW 2025, Shanghai Design Week ran "Design to Wonderland" with 27 Shanghai brands, and young Chinese designers showed at SaloneSatellite — [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/posts/new-generation-flies-made-in-china-flag-at-milan-design-week). Coverage of Salone 2026 describes Chinese exhibitors moving toward "integrated spatial solutions" rather than standalone products (snippet only) — [The Star, Apr 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/04/25/feature-blending-reinterpreting-and-innovating-chinese-designs-in-milan); [China Daily, Apr 2026](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202604/27/WS69ef246ea310d6866eb45c74.html)
- Salone del Mobile is itself investing in China: new Shanghai and Hong Kong events from 2024 and a partnership with Art Basel Hong Kong — [Domus](https://domusweb.it/en/news/gallery/2024/10/15/salone-del-mobile-china-shanghai%E2%80%93hong-kong.html); [Salone press PDF](https://areapress.salonemilano.it/26_26_march_en/Chinese_Press_release_Salone_del_Mobile_collaboration_Art_Basel_Hong_Kong.pdf)
- Correction to the brief: "Mooi-made" probably refers to Moooi, a Dutch brand, not a Chinese one. Moooi's 2025 Salone show centred on sculptural lighting — [My Modern Met](https://mymodernmet.com/moooi-sculptural-lighting/)

**Weaknesses and buyer wariness**
- US Census data as reposted: 2025 US furniture imports from China were $9.75bn, down 31.1%. Total US furniture imports were $52.01bn (down 8.2%). Vietnam led at $14.48bn (up 2.9%). This is a secondary repost, so check against Census — [LB Machinery](https://www.lbmachinery.com/2026/04/25/u-s-furniture-imports-all-9-major-categories-fell-in-2025/)
- China's share of US furniture imports fell from over 50% in 2017 to about 29% by 2023 — [Stacker via LocalNews8](https://localnews8.com/stacker-lifestyle/2025/06/03/how-global-trade-is-reshaping-furniture-pieces/)
- Transshipment: a US industry advocacy piece calls Southeast Asian hubs "extensive transshipment and Chinese investment hubs". This is a risk for any SE Asian supplier that cannot prove origin, and the source is partisan — [Coalition for a Prosperous America](https://prosperousamerica.org/u-s-kitchen-cabinet-industry-needs-support-amid-unrelenting-import-surge/)
- Section 232: a 25% tariff on certain upholstered furniture took effect around 14 October 2025 for all origins, including Thailand. The planned step to 30% on 1 January 2026 was delayed one year, so it may arrive around January 2027. Check against HTSUS Chapter 99 — [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/trump-s-furniture-and-cabinet-tariffs-take-shape); [DFDL](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/legal-and-tax-updates/new-u-s-tariffs-on-asian-furniture-trucks-pharma-october-2025-update-and-compliance-guide/); [Dimerco tariff table Feb 2026](https://lp.dimerco.com/hubfs/Content/US%20Tariff%20Table/US%20Tariff%20Table%20(Feb%2024%2c%202026).pdf). A 50% claim from March 2026 on an unofficial site is UNCORROBORATED — [calcmytariff](https://www.calcmytariff.com/country/thailand/furniture)
- Thai media flagged furniture as a top-10 Thai export at risk from the US tariffs — [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40054782)
- Quality consistency: even pro-China sourcing guides warn that "one good sample doesn't prove batch consistency" and that foam IFD is an often-missed check — [CC Furniture factory verification guide](https://chiuchiufurniture.com/blogs/chiu-blog/how-to-verify-china-furniture-factory); [Foshan FF&E guide](https://chiuchiufurniture.com/blogs/chiu-blog/foshan-hotel-furniture-ffe-procurement-guide)
- Copy culture: one Alibaba listing openly markets a "Roche-Bobois-inspired" sofa from Foshan as a selling point — [Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/sofa-factory-rhodes)

### Inferences
- In the US market, Thailand gets no tariff advantage over China on upholstery under Section 232, and possibly no advantage on reciprocal tariffs either. ASE's case cannot rest on price or tariff arbitrage. It has to rest on trust, provenance, small-batch craft quality and co-creation. Europe, Japan and hospitality FF&E, where Section 232 does not apply, are the more natural targets for a Milan launch.
- The transshipment narrative makes verifiable Thai origin a selling point. ASE can show a 30+ year Thai-owned factory, named long-tenure artisans and traceable material sources such as Doi Tung yarn and Thai latex. A new SE Asian factory backed by Chinese capital cannot show those things.
- Chinese brands are moving up toward design-led "spatial solutions". The gap is therefore not design capability as such. It is human-hand provenance, low-volume bespoke upholstery for hotels and residences, and a calm, non-spectacle brand voice.

### Gaps
- No buyer survey (e.g. Furniture Today, BoH, ISPA, CBI) that quantifies IP, UFLPA or communication concerns for furniture. UFLPA is mostly enforced on cotton, polysilicon and PVC; I found no furniture-specific CBP detention data in this pass.
- No official Foshan/Guangdong 2025 export value.
- Stellar Works' presence at Salone 2025/2026 is unconfirmed. The full articles from The Star and China Daily could not be read.

## Q2. Thai furniture positioning and design-led Thai brands abroad

### Takeaway
Thai design reaches Milan mainly through state programmes run by DITP. These include the Thailand Pavilion / "Host & Home" at Salone, a hospitality-focused presence of about 15 brands in 2026, and "Slow Hand Design" / FRONT 100 at Superstudio Più. The brands that succeeded share three traits: export-OEM roots that turned into an own brand (Deesawat), a signature local material (Yothaka with water hyacinth), or a studio-plus-craft sister brand (THINKK → Thingg). The evidence is awards and placements, not revenue.

### Cited Findings
- DITP / Ministry of Commerce with THACCA ran "Slow Hand Design 2025" ("Cutie Thai: Plaisir d'Amour") and FRONT 100 at Superstudio Più, 7–13 April 2025. 30 of 100 selected emerging talents exhibited — [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/design/slow-hand-design-exhibition-thailand-milan-design-week-04-01-2025/); [Designboom FRONT 100](https://www.designboom.com/design/front-100-thai-talents-milan-design-week-04-02-2025/)
- The Thailand Pavilion at Salone 2025 (Fiera, reportedly Hall 7, booth D18) was framed as a push into European hospitality — [Design Diffusion](https://designdiffusion.com/en/?p=39444); [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055089)
- Salone 2026: Thailand Pavilion "Host & Home", 21–26 April 2026, 15 Thai brands including Deesawat. DITP brought 25 Thai brands to MDW 2026 in total — [DesignWanted](https://designwanted.com/host-home-2026-thai-design-salone-mobile-milan/); [DesignWanted](https://designwanted.com/host-home-2026-exhibition-thai-design-furniture/); [Khaosod English, May 2026](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/2026/05/21/thai-crafts-showcased-at-world-famous-milan-design-week-2026/) (snippet only)
- Earlier Thai MDW presence (2024) was framed around "harmonising chaos" and Thai cultural identity — [STIR](https://www.stirworld.com/stirpad/news/stir-news/harmonising-chaos-exploring-thailand-s-cultural-identity-at-mdw-2024/)
- Deesawat began as an export wooden-furniture manufacturer. It has the PM's Export Award and Japan's Good Design Award, and has worked with international designers (e.g. Samuel Wilkinson's "Parallel") — [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055089); [Dezeen 2023](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/09/22/the-department-of-international-trade-thai-products-design-festivals/)
- Yothaka was founded in 1989 on water hyacinth. It targets Europe and Japan through hotels, resorts and boutiques, and its products appeared in *The White Lotus* — [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055089)
- THINKK Studio has exhibited in Milan, Paris and Taiwan. Its sister brand Thingg focuses on local materials and Thai craft skills — [Vermillion Lifestyle](https://vermillionlifestyle.com/creators/thingg)
- Ayodhya makes "functional and cosmopolitan" products from natural materials and Thai handcraft. Ayodhya and PiN Metal Art were both in DITP's "T-Style: Thai Spirit" collection — [DesignWanted T-Style](https://designwanted.com/t-style-thai-spirit-collection); [DesignWanted](https://designwanted.com/thailand-t-style-aims-for-the-global-design-scene)
- DITP's Talent Thai programme reportedly built "more than 100 Thai brands over eight years" (government PR) — [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055105)
- DEmark (Design Excellence Award) grew out of the PM's Export Award and still runs in 2026 — [DEmark 2026](https://demarkaward.net/en/2026/awards); [Meisterstrasse](https://meisterstrasse.com/en/demark)

### Inferences
- The Thailand Pavilion is already positioned for hospitality. ASE's Rosewood and Kempinski references fit it well, but staying inside a 15-brand national booth risks dilution. A Fuorisalone presentation of its own, or a co-curated one with THINKK as an extension of the BKKDW 2026 work, would carry the "architectural, calm" voice better.
- The THINKK → Thingg model (studio plus craft sister label) is a working precedent for structuring ASE STUDIO and ASE LIVING.

### Gaps
- No revenue or export data for any of these brands. Corner 43, Atelier 2+, Saprang and PDM (Pdm brand) did not appear in the results and are not covered.
- Thai furniture's overall reputation for craftsmanship among European buyers is not supported by any survey I found.

## Q3. Japanese and Scandinavian "craft + industry" precedents

### Takeaway
The Japanese playbook is clear and repeatable. A decades-old regional wood factory relaunches under a famous art director or design director, often Scandinavian (Fukasawa for Maruni in 2008; Norm Architects with Keiji Ashizawa for Karimoku Case from 2019). It uses the language of "industrialised craft" and anchors collections in real architecture projects. Then it shows at Salone year after year.

### Cited Findings
- Maruni was founded in Hiroshima in 1928 and relaunched in 2008 under art director Naoto Fukasawa. The Hiroshima chair became "a symbol of the company's rejuvenation and its commitment to industrialised craft". Jasper Morrison joined in 2011 (Lightwood), followed by the T chair in 2016. Maruni has shown at Salone for years and marked its 90th anniversary there in 2018. Two Maruni pieces are in the V&A collection — [Indesignlive](https://www.indesignlive.com/people/japanese-design-global-map-maruni/amp); [Dezeen 2018](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/04/08/jasper-morrison-naoto-fukasawa-maruni-milan-design-week/); [Monocle](https://monocle.com/design/maruni-wood-industry-japanese-furniture/); [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1420564)
- Karimoku Case Study (now Karimoku Case) is a sister brand of manufacturer Karimoku. It launched in 2019 at Kinfolk Gallery in Copenhagen with Norm Architects and is named after the Case Study Houses. Its first Salone stand was in 2022, with a Japanese-style home by Ashizawa and Norm. In 2024 it took about 160 m² at Rho, themed "natural contrast", and showed pieces made for real projects (Bellustar Tokyo, Azabu Hills Residence) — [Dezeen 2019](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/05/30/karimoku-case-study-architect-designed-furniture/amp/); [Dezeen 2022](https://www.dezeen.com/2022/06/17/japanese-brands-milan-debuts-focus-european-market-grows/amp/); [ArchiPro](https://archipro.com.au/article/karimoku-case-at-salone-del-mobile-2024-good-form)
- Dezeen in 2022 described Japanese brands in Milan as being in "friendly competition" as the European market grows — [Dezeen 2022](https://www.dezeen.com/2022/06/17/japanese-brands-milan-debuts-focus-european-market-grows/amp/)
- Wallpaper*: "Japanese craftsmanship was a front runner" at MDW 2025 (Muji micro-house, Fukasawa for Maruni) — [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/naoto-fukasawa-childrens-play-sculptures); also [Wallpaper* 2024](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/japanese-design-at-milan-design-week)

### Inferences
- A direct template for ASE: (1) a named design director or curator with European credibility, (2) a Salone set that reproduces a real ASE hospitality or residential interior instead of a product line-up, echoing Karimoku's project-based stand, (3) a "since 1992 / long-tenure artisans" story told as industrialised craft, not folklore.
- Maruni's Morrison brief ("so comfortable it wouldn't need upholstery") is the opposite of ASE's speciality. ASE can claim the upholstery side of the Japanese "considered comfort" lineage, backed by its record exporting to Japan.

### Gaps
- Hida Sangyo and Fritz Hansen were not covered by the results. No metrics found for the commercial lift from these relaunches.

## Q4. Thai materials and crafts with design-world credibility

### Takeaway
Thailand has genuinely differentiating materials that support upholstery and are credible to design audiences: natural latex (world's largest producer), plantation rubberwood, Sakon Nakhon GI indigo, Doi Tung handweaving and recycled-PET weaving, and water hyacinth. The credibility problem is proof. FSC-certified rubber is under 0.5% of planted area, and most Doi Tung sustainability claims are self-reported. ASE should claim only what it can document.

### Cited Findings
- Natural rubber: Thailand produces "over 4 million tons/year" and is the world's largest producer and exporter — [NSTDA](https://www.nstda.or.th/en/news/news-years-2019/parafit-innovative-latex-concentrate-for-the-production-of-mattress-and-pillow.html). It had about 36% of world output in 2017 — [BOI](https://www.boi.go.th/upload/content/Rubber_Industry2018_5bb33471b8292.pdf)
- Thailand is the world's largest producer of plantation rubberwood, and Thai furniture relies almost entirely on it — [FSC Asia-Pacific](https://asiapacific.fsc.org/newsfeed/fsc-welcomes-thai-governments-support-on-certifying-rubber-under-fsc-certification-scheme); [ITTO](https://www.itto.int/files/topics/4901_ext_08_17.pdf); [SCB EIC](https://www.scbeic.com/en/detail/product/2987)
- FSC scarcity: FSC rubber covers about 8,000 ha out of more than 1.6m ha planted (under 0.5%). This is a vendor source (TAT WIN, June 2026) — [TAT WIN](https://tatwin-web.pages.dev/insights/fsc-scarcity-thai-rubber/). Sri Trang claims the first fully integrated FSC natural-rubber supply chain — [Sri Trang Gloves](https://www.sritranggloves.com/zh/updates/sd-core-concept/sustainability-for-supply-chain-and-fair-trade/654/fsc-2)
- Natural latex furniture: Theptex (founded 2014) makes 100% natural latex mattresses and furniture certified to GOLS (Global Organic Latex Standard) and is a Spring Air licensee — [BedTimes](https://bedtimesmagazine.com/2024/07/spring-air-thailand-licensee-theptex-joins-for-natural-latex/)
- Indigo: Sakon Nakhon Indigo Paste and Sakon Nakhon Natural Indigo Fabric are registered GIs. The royal "Donkoi Model" (Princess Sirivannavari) pairs local weavers with contemporary designers. Don Koi indigo cotton was used in Thailand's 2026 Asian Games ceremonial uniforms — [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/life/art-culture/40070967); [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/sakon-nakhon-threads-carry-thai-heritage-to-the-asian-games-565286); [CDD Sustainable Fashion](https://sustainablefashion.cdd.go.th/en/donkoi-model-to-the-global-market-creation/)
- Design-led indigo: Prach "Mann" Niyomkar's Mann Crafts / Man Gardens, with the Indigo Loom House designed by Chat Architects — [Magzter/Architectural Digest "Threads of Place"](https://www.magzter.com/es/stories/architecture/da/THREADS-OF-PLACE). An academic study looked at indigo patterns in furniture design — [TCI ThaiJo](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jaad/article/download/289152/183149/1231092)
- Doi Tung: Onitsuka Tiger Mexico 66 uppers handwoven by Doi Tung artisans from 100% recycled PET — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/life/social-and-lifestyle/2123035/onitsuka-tiger-x-doitung-does-thai-textiles). A Hmong-inspired coat used handwoven 100% upcycled-plastic textile (about 130 bottles) — [The Nation](https://nationthailand.com/lifestyle/fashion/40034720). The DOITUNG EVERYDAY line blends deadstock from Doi Tung's own "high-specification factory that already supplies global brands" with community handwoven cloth — [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/corporate/40064996). DoiTung won the G-Green award (self-reported on the foundation's site) — [Mae Fah Luang Foundation](https://www.maefahluang.org/en/doitung-g-green/)
- Water hyacinth: Yothaka has used it since 1989 at a high-end hotel and resort level — [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055089)
- A 2026 Salone trend roundup lists softness after minimalism, including handloom weaving, quilting, appliqué and plush pile, all directly relevant to upholstery textiles — [Net-a-Porter Porter](https://www.net-a-porter.com/porter/article-970c67b6b6c324d4/lifestyle/interiors/salone-del-mobile)

### Inferences
- A "Thai comfort stack" that avoids kitsch: rubberwood frame, natural-latex fill, Doi Tung or indigo handwoven cover, made by named ASE upholsterers. Every layer is a functional material choice, not ornament. Thai identity comes through materials and process (Brand Bible: "the quiet result of everything being considered"), with no motifs, elephants, gold leaf or temple forms.
- Anti-exoticism rules taken from the precedents above: credit the weaving community and designer by name, as Mann Crafts and Donkoi do; show the process and the people; use natural indigo as a colour within the Material Calmness palette (deep, muted), not as "ethnic pattern".
- Sustainability claims must stay tight (brand rule). "Recycled PET handwoven with Doi Tung" is verifiable through the partnership. "FSC rubberwood" or "GOLS latex" should be used only if ASE actually sources certified material, which is scarce in Thailand. Upcycled leather is described as an ambition and should be shown as R&D, not as a claim.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of Thai lacquer, lipao or Thai silk in recent high-end international furniture. Not researched in depth.
- No source confirms a Thai natural-latex upholstery supplier that is both FSC and GOLS certified.

## Q5. Cultural moment 2026–2027 and Asian design reception in Milan

### Takeaway
The 2026 signals point to material authenticity, tactility, softness after minimalism, and a "human pride" reaction to AI. Asian craft is warmly received in Milan: Japan leads, and Korea runs annual government-backed craft shows. Chinese design is rising, but on scale and spectacle. Thailand's state presence leans toward colour and playfulness ("Cutie Thai"), which leaves a calm, architectural Thai voice unclaimed.

### Cited Findings
- Salone 2026 trend framing: "material substance" at the centre, stressing authenticity, longevity and tactile experience, with raw materials (leather, wood) and textured surfaces. This comes from an agency report, so read it as trend framing — [Empire & Co](https://www.empireandco.com/?p=2010). Softness after "years of stark minimalism" (embroidery, quilting, handloom); counterpoint: colour accents replacing "safe beige" — [Net-a-Porter Porter](https://www.net-a-porter.com/porter/article-970c67b6b6c324d4/lifestyle/interiors/salone-del-mobile)
- AI-era craving for the human hand: Human8's *What Matters 2026* "Human pride" trend; via Stylus, 76% of consumers say creativity, craft and imperfection will always matter more than machine-made art — [Human8](https://www.wearehuman8.com/?p=59448); [Stylus, Feb 2026](https://stylus.com/retail-brand-comms/r-bc-heroing-the-human-made). Dentsu 2026: 64% of consumers feel more drawn to traditional values and ways of living, with growing distrust of generated content — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/consumers-in-2026-will-be-full-of-paradoxes-predicts-dentsu/807008/). Handmade market-size figures conflict ($740bn vs >$1.3tn) and should not be used.
- Japan at MDW 2025: "Japanese craftsmanship was a front runner" — [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/naoto-fukasawa-childrens-play-sculptures). SaloneSatellite 2025 "celebrates craft across continents" — [Wallpaper*](https://wallpaper.com/design-interiors/salonesatellite-milan-design-week-2025)
- Korea: the annual "Korean Craft Show in Milan" (Ministry of Culture and KCDF) showed over 630 works in 2024. Korean press reported "simplicity of Korean crafts praised". SEOUL LIFE 2026 MILAN (soban, ADI Design Museum, 20 April–10 May 2026) — [MCST](https://www.mcst.go.kr/english/policy/pressView.jsp?pSeq=390); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/simplicity-of-korean-crafts-praised-in-milan-fair/10377347); [Fuorisalone](https://www.fuorisalone.it/en/2026/events/7006/SEOUL-LIFE-2026-MILAN-Heritage-Reimagined-Soban); [ITB academic study](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jvad/article/view/24061)
- China: national and city delegations (Shanghai), SaloneSatellite talent, and a shift toward integrated spatial solutions in 2026 — see Q1 sources
- Thai state framing at MDW 2025 was "Cutie Thai: Plaisir d'Amour", described as bold colours and a playful spirit — [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/design/slow-hand-design-exhibition-thailand-milan-design-week-04-01-2025/)

### Inferences
- The Korean model (restraint, a single heritage typology such as soban, museum-grade curation) and the Japanese model (industrialised craft) are both being rewarded. ASE's "A Sense of Ease" sits closer to these than to Thailand's official "playful" register, so ASE can take a distinct, calm position within the Thai presence.
- "Ease" maps directly onto the 2026 wellbeing and tactility signals. Upholstery is the most body-contact category of furniture, which supports a sensory, sit-and-touch installation rather than a display.
- Possible narrative hook for 2027, offered as a suggestion not a finding: "made by hands that have done this for 30 years" as the human-provenance counterpoint to AI and automated mass production.

### Gaps
- No McKinsey or BoF *State of Fashion / Luxury* 2026 data on quiet luxury was retrieved. Quiet luxury is asserted from general knowledge only and should be confirmed.
- No critical (Wallpaper*/Dezeen) review of Thai presentations at MDW 2025/2026 was found. Reception quality is unknown.

## Q6. Thai government and design-ecosystem support

### Takeaway
DITP is the operational channel to Milan (pavilion, Slow Hand Design, FRONT 100, T-Style, Talent Thai, DEmark), and THACCA co-brands under the soft-power policy. CEA runs Bangkok Design Week, where ASE already has a 2026 track record with THINKK.

### Cited Findings
- DITP and THACCA co-organised Slow Hand Design / FRONT 100 at MDW 2025 — [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/design/front-100-thai-talents-milan-design-week-04-02-2025/); DITP programme overview — [DesignWanted DITP](https://designwanted.com/brand/ditp-thai-international-trade-promotion); [The Nation PR](https://www.nationthailand.com/pr-news/40055105)
